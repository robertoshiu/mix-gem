## Section 2: Physics Models — Fluid Mechanics

### Reynolds Flow (`reynolds-flow.ts`)

The slurry film between pad and wafer is modeled as a thin viscous film under the **Reynolds lubrication equation**, discretized radially (exploiting axial symmetry from dual rotation):

```
∂/∂r [r·h³·∂p/∂r] = 6μr·ω·∂h/∂θ + 12μr·∂h/∂t
```

**Simplification for browser:** We discretize the wafer radius into **20 radial nodes** (center → edge). At each node:

- **Gap height** `h(r)` = pad deformation − wafer bow + asperity penetration depth
- **Effective velocity** `V(r) = r × (ω_wafer + ω_platen)` — increases linearly from center to edge
- **Pressure** `p(r)` solved via tridiagonal matrix (Thomas algorithm) — O(n) per step
- **Slurry film thickness** output per node, used by contact model

**Pad grooves:** Modeled as 4 concentric channels where pressure resets to atmospheric. Creates discontinuities in the pressure profile that redistribute slurry — this is what drives center-vs-edge non-uniformity.

### Slurry Chemistry (`slurry-chemistry.ts`)

- **Chemical removal component:** Cu dissolution rate via `R_chem = k₀ × exp(-Ea/RT) × [H⁺]^n` for acidic (Cu step) or `[OH⁻]^n` for alkaline (barrier step)
- **Abrasive transport:** Particle concentration depletes along flow direction (center → edge for inward flow). Depletion factor: `C(r) = C₀ × exp(-k_dep × r / Q)` where Q is flow rate
- **Passivation layer:** Cu forms a soft oxide layer (thickness ∝ pH). This layer is what gets mechanically removed — without it, pure mechanical polishing would scratch. Layer regrowth rate competes with removal rate.

## Section 3: Physics Models — Contact Mechanics

### Greenwood-Williamson Asperity Contact (`contact-model.ts`)

The pad surface is modeled as a statistical distribution of hemispherical asperities:

- **Asperity heights:** Gaussian distribution `φ(z)` with mean `z̄` and std `σ_s` derived from pad conditioning state
- **Real contact area** at separation `d`: `A_real = π·η·R_asp·∫_d^∞ (z-d)·φ(z)dz` where `η` = asperity density (slider), `R_asp` = tip radius
- **Contact pressure:** `P_contact = (4/3)·E*·η·√R_asp·∫_d^∞ (z-d)^1.5·φ(z)dz` where `E*` = composite elastic modulus
- **Key insight:** Real contact area is only ~0.1–1% of nominal area. This is why asperity density matters so much — it directly scales removal rate

The integrals are pre-computed as a lookup table (20 separation values) at init time, then interpolated per step. No runtime numerical integration.

### Viscoelastic Pad Response

The pad doesn't respond instantly — it creeps under sustained load:

- **Kelvin-Voigt model:** `ε(t) = (σ/E)·[1 - exp(-t/τ)]` where `τ` = relaxation time (~2–5s for polyurethane IC1000 pad)
- **Effect:** During ramp-up phase, contact area gradually increases as asperities deform. Steady state reached after ~3τ. This is why Phase 0 (ramp-up) exists — the removal rate is unstable until the pad "seats"
- **Pad glazing preset:** Reduces `σ_s` (asperity height variance) → flatter pad → less real contact → removal rate drops

### Preston Removal with Winkler Foundation (`preston-removal.ts`)

Combines everything into material removal:

- **Local pressure** at each radial node: `P(r) = P_contact(r) + P_fluid(r)` — asperity contact dominates at low speed, fluid pressure dominates at high speed (hydroplaning)
- **Preston equation:** `MRR(r) = k_p × P(r) × V(r)` where `k_p` differs per material (Cu: ~5×10⁻¹⁴, barrier: ~1×10⁻¹⁴, oxide: ~0.3×10⁻¹⁴ cm²/dyne)
- **Pattern-dependent Winkler:** Die-level step height modifies local pad deflection. High features → more pressure → faster removal (self-planarizing). Low features → pad bridges over → dishing

## Section 4: Simulation Engine — 4 Phases & State Machine

### Phases (`simulation-engine.ts`)

200 steps total, `dt = 0.5s` per step (100s total process time):

| Phase | Steps | Duration | What happens |
|-------|-------|----------|-------------|
| `ramp-up` | 0–19 | 10s | Pad wetting, viscoelastic creep settling, slurry fills grooves. MRR ramps from 0 to steady-state. |
| `bulk-cu` | 20–119 | 50s | Acidic slurry (pH 4), high down-force. Cu removed at ~500 nm/min. Dishing begins as Cu clears in sparse areas first. |
| `barrier` | 120–169 | 25s | Slurry swap to alkaline (pH 10), reduced pressure. Ta/TaN removed at ~50 nm/min. Erosion accumulates in dense areas. Selectivity to oxide is critical. |
| `buff` | 170–199 | 15s | Ultra-low pressure (0.5 PSI), low speed. Surface roughness reduction, micro-scratch removal. Minimal material removal. |

### Phase Transitions

At each boundary the engine applies a **slurry swap** — chemistry parameters (pH, abrasive concentration, `k_p`) switch to phase-appropriate values. The viscoelastic pad state carries over (no reset), but contact conditions change due to new pressure setpoint. This creates a visible transient in the metrics at each transition.

## Section 2: Physics — ZBL Potential & Stopping Power

### ZBL Screened Coulomb Potential (`zbl-potential.ts`)

The interaction between an incoming ion and a target atom is modeled as a screened Coulomb potential — the nuclear charge is shielded by surrounding electrons:

```
V(r) = (Z1 * Z2 * e²) / (4π * ε0 * r) * φ(r/a)
```

Where `φ(x)` is the ZBL universal screening function:
```
φ(x) = 0.1818*exp(-3.2x) + 0.5099*exp(-0.9423x) + 0.2802*exp(-0.4029x) + 0.02817*exp(-0.2016x)
```

The screening length `a = 0.8854 * a0 / (Z1^0.23 + Z2^0.23)` where a0 is the Bohr radius. This determines the scattering angle for each binary collision — heavier ions (As, Z=33) scatter less per collision than light ions (B, Z=5), so they travel straighter but deposit more energy per collision.

### Stopping Power (`stopping-power.ts`)

Two energy loss mechanisms operate simultaneously:

- **Nuclear stopping** `Sn(E)`: Energy transferred to target atom nuclei via ZBL collisions. Dominates at low energies (<10 keV/amu). Creates lattice damage (displaced atoms). Computed from the ZBL scattering cross-section via the "magic formula" fit.

- **Electronic stopping** `Se(E)`: Continuous energy loss to target electrons (like friction). Dominates at high energies. Modeled via Lindhard-Scharff: `Se = k * sqrt(E)` where k depends on Z1, Z2, and target density. No lattice damage — just slows the ion.

The module exports `computeScatteringAngle(E, impactParam, Z1, Z2, M1, M2)` and `computeElectronicLoss(E, dx, material)`. These are called per-collision by the BCA engine.

## Section 3: Physics — BCA Engine & Channeling

### Binary Collision Approximation (`bca-engine.ts`)

The core of the simulation. Traces a single ion through the target:

1. **Free flight**: Ion travels a distance `λ` (mean free path) determined by target atomic density: `λ = 1 / (n * π * r_max²)` where `n` is atomic density and `r_max` is the maximum impact parameter considered.

2. **Collision partner selection**: Random impact parameter `p` from uniform distribution [0, p_max]. In crystalline regions, the impact parameter is biased toward lattice site positions (enabling channeling). In amorphous regions, purely random.

3. **Scattering**: ZBL potential determines the scattering angle `θ` in the center-of-mass frame. Energy transfer to target atom: `T = 4*M1*M2/(M1+M2)² * E * sin²(θ/2)`. If `T > Ed` (displacement energy, ~15 eV for Si), the target atom is knocked off its lattice site → vacancy + interstitial pair (Frenkel defect).

4. **Electronic loss**: Between collisions, ion loses energy continuously: `ΔE_e = Se(E) * Δx`. Applied along the free-flight path.

5. **Cascade tracking**: If a recoiling target atom has enough energy (`T > Ed`), it becomes a secondary projectile tracked recursively. This creates branching damage cascades — especially dramatic for heavy ions (As creates ~1000 Frenkel pairs per ion; B creates ~10).

6. **Termination**: Ion stops when `E < E_cutoff` (5 eV). Its final (x, y, z) position is recorded.

The engine outputs per ion: `IonTrajectory { points: Vec3[], collisions: CollisionEvent[], finalPosition: Vec3, recoilCascades: Vec3[][] }`.

### Channeling Model (`channeling-model.ts`)

When an ion's velocity aligns within a critical angle `ψ_c` of a crystal axis or plane, it enters a "channel" — guided between rows of atoms by a series of correlated small-angle deflections instead of random hard collisions:

- **Critical angle** (Lindhard): `ψ_c = sqrt(2 * Z1 * Z2 * e² / (E * d))` where `d` is the atomic row spacing. Higher energy → smaller critical angle → harder to channel.

- **Channel detection**: At each step, compute angle between ion velocity and nearest <110>, <100>, <111> axis. If angle < `ψ_c`, ion enters channeled mode.

- **Channeled flight**: Ion experiences only electronic stopping (no nuclear collisions). It oscillates between channel walls with wavelength `λ_ch`. Can be "dechanneled" by: (a) encountering an amorphous pocket, (b) electronic scattering accumulation, (c) thermal vibration of lattice atoms (scaled by substrate temperature via Debye model).

- **Effect on profile**: Channeled ions penetrate 2-5× deeper than random-trajectory ions, creating a characteristic "channeling tail" in the depth profile. This is the primary reason Monte Carlo is needed instead of analytical Gaussian models.

- **Tilt/twist angles**: The user parameters `tilt` (angle from surface normal) and `twist` (rotation around normal) control the beam orientation relative to the crystal. 7° tilt is the standard "off-channel" condition; 0° tilt into <100> Si produces dramatic channeling.

## Section 4: Physics — Damage Model & Monte Carlo Orchestrator

### Damage Model (`damage-model.ts`)

Tracks the cumulative lattice damage state as ions accumulate:

- **Vacancy map**: 1D array binned by depth (same bins as depth profile). Each Frenkel defect (vacancy + interstitial) created by nuclear collisions increments the local bin. Stored as vacancies/cm³.

- **Amorphization threshold**: When local vacancy density exceeds a critical concentration `n_amorph` (~5×10²¹ cm⁻³ for Si, tunable via slider), that depth bin transitions from crystalline to amorphous. This is the key dose-dependent feedback loop.

- **Channeling suppression**: Amorphous regions block channeling — ions entering an amorphous pocket scatter randomly. At high doses, the surface layer amorphizes first (most damage), progressively deepening. This "self-amorphization" causes the depth profile to evolve from channeled (deep tail) at low dose to Gaussian-like (no tail) at high dose.

- **Temperature-dependent annealing**: At elevated substrate temperatures, Frenkel pairs recombine at rate `R_anneal = k0 * exp(-Ea_anneal / kT) * n_vacancy`. This competes with damage creation — hot implants (>400°C) can maintain crystallinity even at high doses. The annealing rate slider exposes this.

- **Pre-amorphization (PAI)**: The damage map can be initialized with a pre-existing amorphous layer (used by the PAI preset). This deliberately suppresses channeling for subsequent light-ion implants (e.g., Ge pre-amorphization before B implant for shallow junctions).

### Monte Carlo Orchestrator (`monte-carlo.ts`)

Runs the ensemble of N ions and produces statistical outputs:

- **Batch execution**: Ions simulated in batches of 10-50. After each batch, depth profile histogram and damage map are updated. This enables the step-based timeline UI.

- **Depth profile binning**: Substrate divided into 200 depth bins (0 to `max_depth`). Each stopped ion increments its depth bin. Lateral position binned into separate histogram for lateral straggle.

- **Multi-layer handling**: When an ion crosses a material interface (e.g., SiO₂→Si), the stopping power coefficients switch to the new material's Z2, M2, density. Interface scattering adds a small random angular kick.

- **Statistics computed per batch**: Rp (mean projected range), ΔRp (straggle = std dev), Xj (junction depth at 1×10¹⁷ cm⁻³), Cp (peak concentration), channeling tail depth, damage peak, lateral straggle, retained dose fraction (ions that stopped in substrate vs. backscattered out of surface).

- **Backscattering**: Ions that reverse direction and exit the surface are counted but not plotted in the depth profile. The retained dose fraction metric captures this — heavy ions in light targets (As in Si) have ~0% backscatter; light ions in heavy targets or at high tilt can lose 5-15%.

## Section 2: Physics Module 1 — Point Defect Model (`point-defects.ts`)

**Purpose:** Track vacancy (V) and interstitial (I) concentrations across depth, governing all dopant transport.

**Equilibrium concentrations** follow Arrhenius:
```
C_V*(T) = C_V0 · exp(-E_fV / kT)
C_I*(T) = C_I0 · exp(-E_fI / kT)
```
where `E_fV ≈ 2.0 eV`, `E_fI ≈ 3.0 eV` are formation energies, `C_V0, C_I0 ≈ 5×10²² cm⁻³` (silicon lattice density).

**Supersaturation ratio** drives TED:
```
S_I = C_I / C_I*(T)    // interstitial supersaturation
S_V = C_V / C_V*(T)    // vacancy supersaturation
```

**Bulk recombination** (V + I → null) via Gilmer-type rate:
```
R_IV = k_IV(T) · (C_I · C_V - C_I* · C_V*)
k_IV = 4π · (D_I + D_V) · a₀ · exp(-E_rec/kT)
```
where `a₀ ≈ 0.235 nm` (Si lattice spacing), `E_rec ≈ 0.5 eV`.

**Surface boundary conditions:** At SiO₂/Si interface, interstitial injection during oxidation (OED):
```
J_I_surface = k_ox · oxidation_rate(T, ambient)
```
where `k_ox` scales with wet vs dry O₂. Free surface (no oxide) acts as perfect sink: `C_I = C_I*`, `C_V = C_V*`.

**{311} defect dissolution** (TED source): Excess interstitials from implant damage stored in {311} rod-like defects, released with time constant:
```
dN_311/dt = -N_311 / τ_311(T)
τ_311(T) = τ₀ · exp(E_311/kT)     // E_311 ≈ 3.6 eV
```
Released interstitials feed into `C_I` field, driving transient enhanced diffusion.

## Section 3: Physics Module 2 — Diffusivity Model (`diffusivity.ts`)

**Purpose:** Compute effective dopant diffusivity `D_eff(x)` at each depth bin, accounting for charge state, Fermi level, and point defect supersaturation.

**Charge-state-dependent diffusivity** — each dopant-defect pair has contributions from neutral and charged states:
```
D_total = D_I + D_V

D_I = h_I · [D_I⁰ + D_I⁺·(p/n_i) + D_I⁻·(n/n_i) + D_I²⁻·(n/n_i)²]
D_V = h_V · [D_V⁰ + D_V⁺·(p/n_i) + D_V⁻·(n/n_i) + D_V²⁻·(n/n_i)²]
```
where `h_I = C_I/C_I*` (interstitial supersaturation), `h_V = C_V/C_V*`, and each `D_X^q = D₀_X^q · exp(-E_X^q / kT)`.

**Intrinsic carrier concentration:**
```
n_i(T) = 3.87×10¹⁶ · T^(3/2) · exp(-0.605 eV / kT)   // cm⁻³
```

**Fermi level / carrier concentrations** from local dopant via charge neutrality. For donor concentration `N_D` or acceptor `N_A`:
```
n = (N_D - N_A)/2 + √[(N_D - N_A)²/4 + n_i²]    // n-type net
p = n_i² / n
```

**Dopant parameter database** (6 species × up to 8 Arrhenius pairs):

| Species | Mechanism | Key D₀ (cm²/s) | Ea (eV) | Notes |
|---------|-----------|-----------------|---------|-------|
| **B** | Interstitial-dominated | D_I⁰=0.037, D_I⁺=0.72 | 3.46, 3.46 | f_I≈1.0, TED-sensitive |
| **P** | Dual (V+I) | D_V⁻=3.85, D_I⁰=3.85 | 3.66, 3.66 | f_I≈0.5, kick-out+Frank-Turnbull |
| **As** | Vacancy-dominated | D_V⁰=0.066, D_V⁻=12.0 | 3.44, 4.05 | f_V≈0.7, clusters above 2×10²⁰ |
| **Sb** | Pure vacancy | D_V⁰=0.214, D_V⁻=15.0 | 3.65, 4.08 | f_V≈1.0, no TED |
| **In** | Interstitial-dominated | D_I⁰=0.6, D_I⁻=1.2 | 3.5, 3.9 | Deep acceptor, retrograde wells |
| **Ge** | Mixed (strain marker) | D_I⁰=6.2, D_V⁰=0.28 | 5.28, 4.65 | Minimal electrical activity |

**Clustering model** — at high concentration (above solid solubility `C_sol(T)`), dopant forms inactive clusters:
```
C_active = C_sol(T) · [1 - exp(-C_total/C_sol(T))]   // saturating activation
C_clustered = C_total - C_active
```
Clustered dopant is immobile (`D = 0`), releasing slowly as active dopant diffuses away.

## Section 4: Physics Module 3 — Thermal Profile Generator (`thermal-profile.ts`)

**Purpose:** Generate the temperature-vs-time curve `T(t)` for each of the five thermal process modes. This drives the diffusion solver — the shape of T(t) determines how much diffusion occurs.

**Five thermal modes:**

**1. Furnace Anneal** (minutes timescale):
```
Ramp:  T(t) = T_ambient + rampRate · t           // 5-15 °C/min
Soak:  T(t) = T_peak                              // 30-120 min hold
Cool:  T(t) = T_peak - coolRate · (t - t_soak)    // 2-5 °C/min natural cool
```
Typical: 900-1100°C, 30-60 min soak. Largest thermal budget.

**2. RTA (Rapid Thermal Anneal)** (seconds):
```
Ramp:  T(t) = T_ambient + rampRate · t           // 50-200 °C/s lamp heating
Soak:  T(t) = T_peak                              // 1-60 s hold
Cool:  T(t) = T_peak · exp(-t/τ_cool)            // τ_cool ≈ 5-20 s radiative
```
Typical: 950-1050°C, 10-30 s. Standard dopant activation.

**3. Spike Anneal** (sub-second):
```
T(t) = T_ambient + (T_peak - T_ambient) · exp(-(t - t_peak)² / (2σ²))
σ = FWHM / 2.355                                  // FWHM ≈ 0.5-2 s
```
Gaussian profile, no soak plateau. Typical: 1050-1100°C peak. Minimal diffusion.

**4. Flash Anneal** (milliseconds):
```
T(t) = T_preheat + (T_peak - T_preheat) · exp(-t/τ_flash)
τ_flash ≈ 0.5-3 ms                                // xenon arc lamp decay
T_preheat ≈ 600-800°C (wafer pre-heated by backside lamp)
```
Typical: T_peak 1200-1350°C for 1-3 ms. Ultra-shallow junction preservation.

**5. Laser Anneal** (microseconds):
```
T(t) = T_base + (T_peak - T_base) · rect(t/τ_laser) · exp(-z/δ_thermal)
τ_laser ≈ 0.1-1 ms (scan dwell time)
δ_thermal = √(α · τ_laser)                        // thermal diffusion length
α_Si ≈ 0.9 cm²/s (thermal diffusivity)
```
Near-surface only: `δ_thermal ≈ 10-30 μm`. T_peak up to 1410°C (Si melting). Depth-dependent temperature is unique to this mode — top bins see full temperature while deep bins stay near T_base.

**Thermal budget computation:**
```
Dt = Σ_steps D_eff(T(t)) · Δt                     // cumulative diffusion length²
L_diff = √(Dt)                                     // characteristic diffusion length
```

**Time discretization** adapts to mode:
```
Furnace:  Δt = 1-10 s     (totalSteps=200 → covers ~30 min)
RTA:      Δt = 50-200 ms  (totalSteps=200 → covers ~20 s)
Spike:    Δt = 5-20 ms    (totalSteps=200 → covers ~2 s)
Flash:    Δt = 10-50 μs   (totalSteps=200 → covers ~5 ms)
Laser:    Δt = 0.5-5 μs   (totalSteps=200 → covers ~0.5 ms)
```

## Section 5: Physics Module 4 — PDE Diffusion Solver (`diffusion-solver.ts`)

**Purpose:** Core numerical engine. Solves the coupled 1D diffusion PDEs for dopant concentration and point defects on the depth grid at each thermal timestep.

**Governing equation** (extended Fick's second law):
```
∂C/∂t = ∂/∂x [D_eff(C,T,n_i,V,I) · ∂C/∂x] + G(x,t) - L(x,t)
```
where `G` = generation (cluster dissolution, {311} release), `L` = loss (clustering, precipitation).

**Crank-Nicolson semi-implicit scheme** — unconditionally stable for any Δt, second-order accurate in both space and time:
```
(C^(n+1) - C^n) / Δt = 0.5 · [L(C^(n+1)) + L(C^n)]
```
where `L(C) = ∂/∂x[D·∂C/∂x]` discretized as tridiagonal system:
```
L(C)_i = D_(i+½)·(C_(i+1) - C_i)/Δx² - D_(i-½)·(C_i - C_(i-1))/Δx²
```
Half-point diffusivities: `D_(i+½) = 0.5·(D_i + D_(i+1))`.

**Tridiagonal solver** (Thomas algorithm) — O(N) per species per timestep:
```
a_i · C_(i-1)^(n+1) + b_i · C_i^(n+1) + c_i · C_(i+1)^(n+1) = d_i

where:
  r_i = D_(i+½) · Δt / (2·Δx²)
  a_i = -r_(i-½)
  c_i = -r_(i+½)
  b_i = 1 + r_(i-½) + r_(i+½)
  d_i = C_i^n + r_(i-½)·C_(i-1)^n + (- r_(i-½) - r_(i+½))·C_i^n + r_(i+½)·C_(i+1)^n  // explicit half
```

**Boundary conditions:**
- Surface (i=0): Reflective (`∂C/∂x = 0`) or segregation sink depending on oxide presence
- Substrate (i=N-1): Zero-flux (`∂C/∂x = 0`) — deep bulk unaffected

**Coupled solve order** per timestep:
1. Update temperature from thermal profile → `T, T(z)`
2. Compute `n_i(T)`, carrier concentrations from current dopant profile
3. Compute `D_eff` per bin from diffusivity model (charge state + supersaturation)
4. Step point defects ({311} dissolution → interstitial release → recombination)
5. Solve dopant diffusion PDE (Crank-Nicolson tridiagonal)
6. Apply clustering (cap active concentration at solid solubility)
7. Update segregation at interfaces
8. Snapshot all profiles into StepState

**Segregation at SiO₂/Si interface:**
```
C_oxide / C_silicon = m(T)    // segregation coefficient
m_B ≈ 0.3 (B piles into oxide)
m_P ≈ 10  (P piles into silicon)
m_As ≈ 10 (As piles into silicon)
```
Implemented as flux boundary: `J = k_seg · (C_Si - C_Ox/m)`.

| **Sheet resistance Rs** | `1 / ∫q·μ(C)·C_active(x)dx` with mobility model | Ω/□ |
| **Peak concentration** | `max(C_active)` | cm⁻³ |
| **Thermal budget Dt** | Cumulative `Σ D_eff·Δt` | cm² |
| **Activation fraction** | `∫C_active dx / ∫C_total dx` | % |
| **Interstitial supersaturation** | `max(C_I / C_I*)` peak value | ratio |
| **Profile abruptness** | `Δx / Δ(log₁₀C)` at junction | nm/decade |
| **Segregation ratio** | `C(x=0) / C(x=2nm)` surface pile-up | ratio |
| **Vacancy concentration** | `max(C_V / C_V*)` normalized peak | ratio |
| **Diffusion length** | `√(Dt)` characteristic length | nm |

**Mobility model** (for sheet resistance, Masetti model):
```
μ(C) = μ_min + (μ_max - μ_min) / (1 + (C/C_ref)^α) - μ₁/(1 + (C_ref2/C)^β)
```
with published Masetti parameters for electrons and holes.

## Section 8: Babylon.js 3D Wafer Slab Scene (`DiffusionScene.tsx`)

**Purpose:** Left panel — 3D volumetric visualization of dopant diffusion evolving in real-time.

**Wafer slab geometry:** A 100×20×200 unit box (width × thickness × depth) representing a silicon cross-section. The depth axis (Y, pointing down) maps to the 200-bin depth grid. The slab is semi-transparent to reveal internal concentration.

**Dopant concentration volumetric color:** The slab surface is subdivided into horizontal strips (one per ~5 bins). Each strip's color maps log₁₀(C) to a heatmap:
```
log₁₀(C) ≥ 20 → bright amber #F59E0B (high conc)
log₁₀(C) ≈ 17 → orange #EA580C
log₁₀(C) ≈ 14 → dark red #7F1D1D
log₁₀(C) < 14 → substrate blue #1E3A5F (background)
```
Implemented as `StandardMaterial` per strip with `diffuseColor` updated in render loop from `propsRef.current.step.dopantProfile[]`.

**Junction depth isosurface:** A thin glowing plane at Y = Xj depth, rendered as a flat box with emissive amber material + alpha 0.6. Moves up/down as junction evolves. Dashed line label "Xj" using Babylon.js GUI `AdvancedDynamicTexture`.

**Point defect particles:** Two particle systems:
- **Interstitials** — small cyan spheres (`diameter: 1.5`), count proportional to `max(C_I/C_I*)`. Positioned randomly within high-supersaturation depth bins. Gentle upward drift animation (interstitials migrating to surface).
- **Vacancies** — small purple spheres (`diameter: 1.5`), count proportional to `max(C_V/C_V*)`. Static or slow random walk.
- Pool of 50 reusable spheres per species, visibility toggled per step.

**Layer stack visualization:**
- Screen oxide layer (if thickness > 0): semi-transparent violet box on top of slab
- Segregation zone: thin orange highlight at oxide/silicon interface when segregation ratio > 1.5

**Temperature overlay:** Left edge of slab shows a vertical temperature gradient bar, colored blue→red mapping T(z) profile. Particularly dramatic for laser mode where only top bins glow red.

**Camera:** `ArcRotateCamera` at 45° looking at slab center. Limits: radius 100-800, beta 0.15-0.85π.

**Thermal mode indicator:** Small text overlay in top-left showing current mode name, time elapsed, and phase (ramp/soak/cool/pulse) with phase-colored background.
